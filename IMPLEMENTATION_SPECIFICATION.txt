### 2.1.5 Circadian Rhythm Model
Implement a master circadian oscillator using a coupled set of differential equations representing the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN):

```
dS/dt = (1/tau_c) * (S_max * (1 - S) * activator - S * inhibitor + noise_S)
```

Where:
- `S` = circadian phase signal [0, 1]
- `tau_c` = period (~24.0 ± 0.5 hours, patient-specific)
- `activator` = light-dependent input (sinusoidal with 24h period, zero at night)
- `inhibitor` = melatonin-dependent feedback
- `noise_S` = small stochastic perturbation

The circadian signal must modulate:
- Heart rate baseline (nadir at 04:00, peak at 10:00)
- Blood pressure (similar pattern, amplitude ±10 mmHg systolic)
- Temperature (nadir at 05:00, peak at 18:00)
- Cortisol proxy (peaks at 08:00)
- HRV (higher parasympathetic tone during sleep)
- Respiratory rate (lower during sleep)
- Metabolic rate (lower during sleep)

### 2.1.6 Activity Cycle Model
Implement a discrete-state Markov activity model:
- States: ["sleeping", "resting", "light_activity", "moderate_activity", "vigorous_activity"]
- Transition matrix: patient-specific, influenced by occupation, fitness, age
- Each state has associated physiological effects:
  - Sleep: HR drops, BP drops, parasympathetic dominance, temperature drops
  - Resting: Baseline values
  - Light activity: HR +10-30%, BP +5-15%
  - Moderate activity: HR +30-60%, BP +15-30%, respiration +50-100%
  - Vigorous activity: HR +60-100%, BP +30-50%, respiration +100-200%, may trigger arrhythmia in susceptible patients

### 2.1.7 Autonomic Nervous System (ANS) Model
Implement a coupled oscillator model:
- Sympathetic tone: Driven by stress, activity, pain, volume status
- Parasympathetic tone: Driven by circadian phase, baroreceptor feedback, respiratory phase
- Respiratory Sinus Arrhythmia (RSA): HR modulation synchronized with respiration:
  - HR increases during inspiration (sympathetic withdrawal + vagal withdrawal)
  - HR decreases during expiration (vagal rebound)
  - RSA amplitude: modulated by fitness level and autonomic balance
- Baroreceptor reflex: BP deviations trigger compensatory HR changes with appropriate delay (0.5-2 seconds)
- LF power (0.04-0.15 Hz): Reflects both sympathetic and parasympathetic activity
- HF power (0.15-0.40 Hz): Primarily parasympathetic (respiratory frequency)
- LF/HF ratio: Sympathovagal balance indicator

### 2.1.8 Respiratory Coupling Model
- Respiratory rate: Modulated by circadian phase, activity, metabolic demands
- Tidal volume: Linked to metabolic rate and activity
- Respiratory variability: Natural breath-to-breath variability (Coefficient of variation ~15-25%)
- Cheyne-Stokes pattern: Emerges in severe heart failure (crescendo-decrescendo pattern)
- Kussmaul breathing: Emerges in metabolic acidosis (diabetic ketoacidosis, sepsis)
- Biot's breathing: Irregular pattern in brainstem dysfunction

### 2.1.9 Thermoregulation Model
- Core temperature: Modulated by metabolic rate, ambient temperature, activity, infection
- Skin temperature: Peripheral vasodilation/constriction, circadian modulation
- Fever response: When inflammatory_state > 0.5, core temp rises proportional to inflammation
- Heat dissipation: Proportional to skin-core temperature gradient

### 2.1.10 Hydration and Metabolic Model
- Fluid intake: Markov-modulated (drinking events)
- Fluid loss: Renal (temperature-dependent), respiratory, insensible
- Blood osmolality: Inversely related to hydration
- Electrolyte concentrations: Affected by medications, renal function, fluid balance
- Blood glucose: Modulated by meals (3x daily), insulin sensitivity, diabetes status
- Metabolic rate: Basal (age, sex, BMI dependent) × activity multiplier × thyroid status

### 2.2.2 Disease Types and Pathophysiological Models

Each disease must be implemented as a stochastic differential equation system affecting specific latent state variables. Disease progression must be PROBABILISTIC, not deterministic.

#### A. Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) Family

**Unstable Angina / NSTEMI:**
- Pathophysiology: Partial coronary occlusion, subendocardial ischemia
- Latent state effects:
  - Progressive increase in `ischemic_burden` [0 → 0.6]
  - Troponin elevation: NSTEMI shows gradual rise (0 → 50 ng/L over 6-12h), unstable angina shows no significant elevation
  - ST-segment depression: Progressive, depth proportional to ischemic_burden
  - HR response: Sympathetic activation, HR increases 10-30%
  - HRV: Progressive reduction in HF power (parasympathetic withdrawal)
  - Risk of progression to STEMI: 5-15% within 48 hours
  - Risk of progression to VT/VF: 2-5%

**STEMI:**
- Pathophysiology: Complete coronary occlusion, transmural ischemia
- Latent state effects:
  - Rapid increase in `ischemic_burden` [0.4 → 1.0] over 30-60 minutes
  - Troponin: Rapid rise (0 → 200+ ng/L over 2-4 hours)
  - ST-segment elevation: Proportional to territory, leads I/aVL (LAD), II/III/aVF (RCA), V1-V4 (LAD proximal)
  - Q-waves develop after 2-6 hours (necrosis marker)
  - Killip class progression: I → II → III → IV
  - Risk of cardiogenic shock: 5-8%
  - Risk of VT/VF in first 24h: 10-15%
  - Risk of OHCA: 8-12% in first 24 hours
  - Ejection fraction: Acute reduction proportional to territory size

#### B. Heart Failure Family

**Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM):**
- Pathophysiology: Ventricular dilation, systolic dysfunction
- Progressive reduction in `ejection_fraction`: [0.50 → 0.15] over months
- Compensatory neurohormonal activation: RAAS, sympathetic nervous system
- BNP elevation: Proportional to filling pressures
- Edema: When fluid retention exceeds excretion capacity
- Arrhythmia risk: Progressive increase with EF reduction
- OHCA risk: Increases dramatically when EF < 0.25

**Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM):**
- Pathophysiology: Asymmetric septal hypertrophy, diastolic dysfunction
- Increased myocardial irritability: Baseline elevated
- LVOT obstruction: Dynamic, affected by preload/afterload
- Ventricular arrhythmia: Risk 1-2% per year, higher with severe hypertrophy
- Sudden cardiac arrest risk: 0.5-1% per year overall, higher in certain genotypes
- Wall thickness: 15-35mm (normal < 12mm)

**Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy (ACM/ARVC):**
- Pathophysiology: Fibrofatty replacement of right ventricle
- Progressive RV dilation and dysfunction
- epsilon waves on ECG
- T-wave inversions in V1-V3
- Ventricular arrhythmia: LBBB morphology VT
- Exercise-triggered arrhythmias
- Progressive: May involve LV in advanced stages

#### C. Primary Electrical Disease (Channelopathies)

**Brugada Syndrome:**
- Pathophysiology: Sodium channel dysfunction (SCN5A mutations)
- ECG pattern: Coved ST elevation in V1-V2
- Brugada type 1: Diagnostic coved pattern
- Arrhythmia risk: Highest during sleep/rest (vagally mediated)
- Fever-triggered: Risk increases with hyperthermia
- Drug-triggered: Sodium channel blockers can unmask
- VF risk: 1-2% per year in symptomatic patients
- OHCA mechanism: Polymorphic VT → VF

**Long QT Syndrome (LQTS):**
- Pathophysiology: Prolonged ventricular repolarization
- QTc prolongation: >470ms (male), >480ms (female) diagnostic
- Torsades de Pointes: Polymorphic VT triggered by pause-dependent mechanisms
- Triggers: Sudden loud noise (LQT1), emotional stress/exercise (LQT2), sleep (LQT3)
- Risk: Highest in first year of life and at puberty
- ECG manifestation: Prolonged QT, T-wave alternans, notched T-waves
- Drug interactions: Many drugs prolong QT (sotalol, amiodarone, etc.)

**Short QT Syndrome:**
- Pathophysiology: Accelerated repolarization
- QTc < 340ms diagnostic
- Atrial and ventricular arrhythmia risk
- VF risk: ~1% per year
- ECG: Short QT, tall peaked T-waves, absent ST segment

**Catecholaminergic Polymorphic VT (CPVT):**
- Pathophysiology: Abnormal calcium handling in cardiomyocytes
- Bidirectional VT during exercise or catecholamine surge
- Polymorphic VT at higher heart rates
- Normal resting ECG
- Risk: Highest during physical exertion or emotional stress
- OHCA risk: 30% of untreated patients have cardiac arrest by age 30

#### D. Tachyarrhythmias

**Atrial Fibrillation (AF):**
- Pathophysiology: Chaotic atrial electrical activity
- Irregularly irregular ventricular response
- Rapid ventricular response (>100 bpm) vs controlled (<100 bpm)
- Stroke risk: CHA₂DS₂-VASc score dependent
- Tachycardia-mediated cardiomyopathy if chronic and uncontrolled
- Hemodynamic effects: Loss of atrial kick reduces cardiac output 15-25%
- May be paroxysmal, persistent, or permanent

**Ventricular Tachycardia (VT):**
- Pathophysiology: Reentrant circuit in ventricle
- Monomorphic VT: Regular, single morphology
- Polymorphic VT: Variable morphology, often degenerates to VF
- Hemodynamic compromise: Depends on rate and cardiac function
- Can occur without structural heart disease (idiopathic)
- Risk factors: Prior MI, cardiomyopathy, channelopathies
- Pre-arrest VT: Often the immediate precursor to OHCA

**Ventricular Fibrillation (VF):**
- Pathophysiology: Chaotic ventricular electrical activity
- No organized cardiac output → pulseless
- Fatal within minutes without defibrillation
- OHCA mechanism in ~80% of shockable arrests
- ECG: Disorganized, irregular waveform, no identifiable QRS

#### E. Conduction Disorders

**Complete Heart Block (Third-degree AV block):**
- Pathophysiology: Complete failure of AV conduction
- Escape rhythm: Junctional (40-60 bpm) or ventricular (20-40 bpm)
- Hemodynamic: Severely reduced cardiac output
- Syncope risk: High, especially with slow escape rhythm
- May be progressive (Mobitz I → Mobitz II → complete)
- Causes: Degenerative fibrosis, inferior MI, Lyme disease

#### F. Metabolic and Systemic Emergencies

**Hyperkalemia:**
- Pathophysiology: Elevated serum potassium (>5.5 mmol/L)
- ECG progression: Peaked T-waves → PR prolongation → QRS widening → sine wave → asystole/VF
- Severity grading: Mild (5.5-6.0), Moderate (6.0-7.0), Severe (>7.0)
- Causes: Renal failure, ACE inhibitors, NSAIDs, potassium supplements, tissue breakdown
- OHCA mechanism: Progressive conduction failure → VF or asystole
- Onset: Can be rapid (minutes to hours)

**Hypokalemia:**
- Pathophysiology: Low serum potassium (<3.5 mmol/L)
- ECG changes: ST depression, T-wave flattening, U-waves, QT prolongation
- Arrhythmia risk: Polymorphic VT, torsades de pointes
- Causes: Diuretics, vomiting, diarrhea, poor intake
- Compounded by: Hypomagnesemia

**Pulmonary Embolism (PE):**
- Pathophysiology: Thrombus occluding pulmonary vasculature
- Massive PE: >50% vascular obstruction → right heart failure → cardiovascular collapse
- Submassive PE: RV dysfunction without hemodynamic instability
- ECG changes: Sinus tachycardia, S1Q3T3, right heart strain pattern
- SpO2: Progressive desaturation
- Hemodynamics: Increased pulmonary vascular resistance → RV dilation → LV underfilling → shock
- OHCA mechanism: Obstructive shock → PEA
- Onset: Minutes to hours depending on embolus size

**Respiratory Failure:**
- Pathophysiology: Inadequate oxygenation or ventilation
- Type I: Hypoxemic (PaO2 < 60 mmHg)
- Type II: Hypercapnic (PaCO2 > 50 mmHg)
- ECG effects: Sinus tachycardia, right heart strain in chronic cases
- SpO2: Progressive desaturation
- CO2 retention: Respiratory acidosis → hyperkalemia
- OHCA mechanism: Hypoxia → bradycardia → asystole, or combined with acidosis → VF

**Sepsis:**
- Pathophysiology: Systemic inflammatory response to infection
- Hemodynamic: Vasodilatory shock (warm shock → cold shock progression)
- Tachycardia, tachypnea, fever (or hypothermia in elderly)
- Lactate elevation: >2 mmol/L indicates tissue hypoperfusion
- ECG: Sinus tachycardia, non-specific ST-T changes, QT prolongation
- Organ dysfunction: Myocardial depression (septic cardiomyopathy)
- OHCA mechanism: Multi-organ failure → arrhythmia or PEA

**Drug Toxicity:**
- Tricyclic antidepressant overdose: QRS widening, QT prolongation, VF
- Digoxin toxicity: AV block, bidirectional VT, "salvador dali" T-waves
- Beta-blocker overdose: Bradycardia, conduction block, asystole
- Calcium channel blocker overdose: Bradycardia, hypotension, cardiogenic shock
- Cocaine/amphetamine: Coronary vasospasm, MI, VT/VF, hyperthermia

### 2.2.3 Multiple Simultaneous Diseases
- 30% of patients must have 2+ concurrent disease processes
- Disease interactions must be modeled (e.g., COPD + heart failure = worse outcomes)
- Drug interactions between treatments for different diseases must affect physiology
- The combination of diseases creates emergent risk not captured by any single disease model

### 2.2.4 Stochastic Disease Evolution
Each disease progression follows a stochastic process:
- SDE: `dS = mu(S,t)dt + sigma(S,t)dW` where `W` is a Wiener process
- `mu` = drift term (disease-specific deterministic progression)
- `sigma` = diffusion term (patient-specific variability)
- Disease transitions: Markov chain between stages with transition probabilities dependent on current state
- Collapse events: Triggered when `probability_of_ohca` exceeds a stochastic threshold

```python
    1. The patient's latent state at that timepoint
    2. The future trajectory of the patient
    3. Whether an OHCA event occurs within the pre_arrest_window_hours
    
    The label is NOT the instantaneous probability_of_ohca. It is a 
    derived quantity that represents the forward-looking risk.
    
    Specifically:
    - If OHCA occurs within the window: label = 1.0
    - If OHCA occurs after the window: label = 0.0 (censored)
    - If no OHCA occurs: label = 0.0
    
    The MODEL should learn to predict the latent probability, not 
    binary labels. Binary labels are only used for classification 
    threshold evaluation.
    """
```

For survival analysis, implement right-censored labels:
- Event time: Time from current point to OHCA (if it occurs)
- Censoring indicator: 1 if event occurs, 0 if censored (end of simulation, no event)
- This enables Cox proportional hazards and survival analysis losses

## 2.3 Wearable Sensor Physics Simulation (sensors.py)

### 2.3.1 ECG Signal Generation

Implement a physics-based ECG generator using the McSharry limit-cycle model with extensions:

#### Core ECG Model
The ECG waveform is generated using a system of non-linear ODEs driven by attractive forces on a phase trajectory:

```
dz/dt = -sum_i(a_i * Delta_theta_i * exp(-Delta_theta_i^2 / (2*b_i^2))) - (z - z_0)
dtheta/dt = omega + sum_i(a_i * Delta_theta_i * exp(-Delta_theta_i^2 / (2*b_i^2))) * (z - z_i)
```

Where:
- `theta` = phase angle on the limit cycle [0, 2*pi]
- `z` = vertical deflection (ECG amplitude)
- `omega` = angular velocity (determines heart rate)
- `a_i` = amplitude of Gaussian attractor i (P, Q, R, S, T waves)
- `b_i` = width of Gaussian attractor i
- `z_i` = vertical position of Gaussian attractor i
- `Delta_theta_i = theta - theta_i` (phase difference from attractor center)

#### Extended Parameters per Wave Component
- P-wave: a_P = 0.1-0.25 mV, b_P = 0.05-0.15 rad, theta_P = 0.0-0.3 rad (before QRS)
- Q-wave: a_Q = 0.05-0.15 mV, b_Q = 0.01-0.05 rad, theta_Q = centered at QRS onset
- R-wave: a_R = 0.8-1.5 mV, b_R = 0.01-0.03 rad, theta_R = QRS peak
- S-wave: a_S = 0.1-0.3 mV, b_S = 0.01-0.05 rad, theta_S = just after R
- T-wave: a_T = 0.15-0.4 mV, b_T = 0.1-0.3 rad, theta_T = 1.5-2.5 rad (after QRS)
- U-wave (optional): a_U = 0.02-0.05 mV, b_U = 0.05-0.1 rad

#### HRV Modulation
Heart rate variability is injected by modulating `omega` (angular velocity):

1. **RSA (Respiratory Sinus Arrhythmia):**
   - HR modulation at respiratory frequency (0.15-0.40 Hz)
   - Amplitude: ±3-10 bpm depending on age, fitness, autonomic tone
   - Phase: HR increases during inspiration, decreases during expiration

2. **LF Component (0.04-0.15 Hz):**
   - Blood pressure oscillations (Mayer waves)
   - Modulates HR via baroreceptor reflex
   - Amplitude: ±2-5 bpm

3. **HF Component (0.15-0.40 Hz):**
   - Vagal tone modulation
   - Direct respiratory coupling
   - Amplitude: ±3-10 bpm

4. **Very Low Frequency (VLF, 0.003-0.04 Hz):**
   - Thermoregulatory, RAAS, hormonal oscillations
   - Amplitude: ±1-3 bpm

5. **Ultra-Low Frequency (ULF, <0.003 Hz):**
   - Circadian modulation
   - Amplitude: ±5-15 bpm

The total HR modulation is the sum of all frequency components, with each component's amplitude modulated by the current autonomic tone and patient fitness.

#### Demographic Morphology Modifications

**Age Effects (>65 years):**
- PR interval: 180-220 ms (vs 120-200 ms in young adults)
- QRS duration: 100-120 ms (vs 80-100 ms)
- HRV: Reduced (RMSSD typically <30 ms)
- T-wave: May flatten
- QRS voltage: May decrease
- U-waves: More prominent

**Sex Effects (Female):**
- QTc: 440-470 ms (vs 400-440 ms in males)
- T-wave: Often more prominent in V2-V3
- HR: Higher baseline (5-10 bpm)
- QRS voltage: Slightly lower

**BMI Effects (High BMI):**
- Signal amplitude: 3-6 dB reduction (fat tissue acts as low-pass filter)
- R-peak amplitude: Reduced
- QRS morphology: Rounded peaks (high-frequency attenuation)
- Baseline wander: Increased (motion artifact from soft tissue)
- P-wave: May be difficult to detect
- Overall SNR: Reduced proportionally to BMI

#### Disease-Specific ECG Modifications

**Ischemia:**
- ST depression: Horizontal or downsloping, 0.5-3.0 mm
- ST elevation: Regional, territory-specific
- T-wave inversion: In ischemic territory
- Hyperacute T-waves: EarlySTEMI (tall, peaked, symmetric)
- Q-waves: Pathological if >40 ms or >25% of R-wave amplitude

**Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH):**
- Increased QRS voltage: S in V1 + R in V5/V6 > 35 mm
- ST depression in lateral leads
- T-wave inversion in lateral leads
- Left atrial enlargement: P-terminal force in V1

**Right Ventricular Hypertrophy (RVH):**
- R-wave dominance in V1
- ST depression in V1-V3
- T-wave inversion in V1-V3

**Bundle Branch Blocks:**
- LBBB: QRS >120 ms, broad notched R in I, aVL, V5-V6, QS in V1-V2
- RBBB: QRS >120 ms, rsR' in V1-V2, wide S in I, V6

**Hyperkalemia Progression:**
- K+ 5.5-6.0: Peaked T-waves (narrow, tall, symmetric)
- K+ 6.0-6.5: PR prolongation, P-wave flattening
- K+ 6.5-7.0: QRS widening
- K+ >7.0: Sine wave pattern → VF/asystole

**Hypokalemia:**
- ST depression
- T-wave flattening
- Prominent U-waves
- QT prolongation (pseudo-long QT from U-wave fusion)

### 2.3.2 Accelerometer Signal Generation

Generate 3-axis accelerometer data (X, Y, Z) at 52 Hz:

**Baseline Signal (Standing/Resting):**
- Gravity component: Primarily in vertical axis (Z), magnitude ~9.81 m/s²
- Small postural sway: Low-frequency noise (0.1-0.5 Hz), amplitude 0.01-0.05 g
- Respiration artifact: Slight rhythmic modulation at respiratory frequency

**Activity-Specific Signals:**

*Walking:*
- Dominant axis: Vertical (Z)
- Frequency: 1.5-2.5 Hz (step frequency)
- Amplitude: 0.1-0.3 g (peak-to-peak)
- Harmonics: 2nd and 3rd harmonics present
- Lateral (X) and anterior-posterior (Y): Smaller amplitude, phase-shifted

*Running:*
- Higher amplitude: 0.3-0.8 g vertical
- Higher frequency: 2.5-4.0 Hz
- Greater harmonic content
- More X/Y axis activity (arm swing, body rotation)

*Ascending/Descending Stairs:*
- Asymmetric vertical acceleration pattern
- Lower frequency than level walking (1.0-1.8 Hz)
- Higher amplitude than level walking

*Falling:*
- Sudden high-G vertical acceleration: 3-10 g
- Duration: 0.3-0.8 seconds
- Followed by impact: Very high-G transient (>10 g)
- Post-fall: Near-zero acceleration (motionless)

*Vigorous Arm Movement:*
- Large X and Y axis accelerations
- Less regular than locomotion
- May correlate with exercise-induced arrhythmia triggers

**BMI Scaling:**
- Acceleration amplitude inversely proportional to BMI
- Higher BMI = more soft tissue damping
- Higher frequency components attenuated more
- Scale factor: `1 / (1 + 0.02 * (BMI - 22))`

### 2.3.3 Gyroscope Signal Generation

Generate 3-axis angular velocity data at 52 Hz:
- Primarily zero during rest (small postural sway)
- Walking: Small oscillatory rotations (~1-5 deg/s)
- Running: Larger rotations with step frequency
- Wrist rotation: Large transient angular velocities during gestures
- Fall: Rapid angular velocity change followed by quiescence
- Watch rotation: Intermittent large angular velocity transients

### 2.3.4 PPG Signal Generation

Generate photoplethysmography signal at 50 Hz:
- Waveform shape: Systolic peak + dicrotic notch + diastolic runoff
- Generated using similar Gaussian attractor model as ECG but with PPG-specific morphology
- Heart rate: Synchronized with ECG R-R intervals
- Pulse transit time: 200-300 ms after R-wave (varies with BP)
- Amplitude modulation: Respiratory-induced amplitude variation (10-15%)
- Motion artifact: Correlated with accelerometer signals
- Skin tone effect: Darker skin reduces PPG amplitude by 10-30%
- Perfusion index: 0.02-20%, affected by temperature, vasoconstriction

### 2.3.5 SpO2 Signal Generation

- Derived from PPG signal via Beer-Lambert law approximation
- R-ratio = (AC_red/DC_red) / (AC_ir/DC_ir)
- SpO2 = 110 - 25 * R (simplified calibration curve)
- Normal range: 95-100%
- Desaturation events: Correlated with respiratory events
- Motion artifact: SpO2 becomes unreliable during significant motion
- Update rate: 10 Hz (or every heart beat)

### 2.3.6 Skin Temperature Signal

- Baseline: 33-36°C depending on ambient temperature, body composition
- Circadian modulation: ±0.5°C
- Exercise: Increases by 1-3°C
- Fever: Core temperature elevation → skin temperature follows with delay
- Vasodilation: Skin temperature rises
- Vasoconstriction: Skin temperature drops

### 2.3.7 Respiration Signal

- Derived from thoracic impedance variation or accelerometry-derived respiration
- Respiratory rate: 8-40 breaths/min depending on state
- Tidal volume: 300-600 mL at rest, up to 2-3L during exercise
- Respiratory variability: Natural breath-to-breath variation
- Cheyne-Stokes: Crescendo-decrescendo pattern in severe HF
- Apnea events: Central and obstructive patterns
- Kussmaul: Deep, rapid breathing in acidosis

### 2.3.8 Sensor Artifact Models

**ADC Quantization:**
- Resolution: 12-bit (4096 levels)
- Range: ±3.2 mV for ECG
- Quantization step: 6.4 µV
- Effect: Adds small quantization noise, particularly visible in low-amplitude signals

**Clock Drift:**
- Rate: 5 ppm (parts per million)
- Effect: Accumulates to ~0.43 seconds/day
- Manifestation: Gradual timestamp misalignment between modalities
- Correction: Must be modeled as systematic, not random

**Timestamp Jitter:**
- Distribution: Gaussian, σ = 2 ms
- Effect: Small random timing errors in each sample
- Manifestation: Slight phase noise in periodic signals

**Packet Loss:**
- Rate: 2% of all data packets
- Manifestation: Missing segments of data (50-200 ms gaps)
- Pattern: Bursty (correlated with Bluetooth congestion)

**Bluetooth Latency:**
- Mean: 50 ms
- Std: 15 ms
- Distribution: Log-normal (right-skewed)
- Effect: Variable delay between measurement and recording

**Lead Vector Changes (ECG):**
- Watch rotation causes changes in effective lead vector
- Slow rotation: Gradual amplitude change
- Quick rotation: Sudden amplitude/phase change
- Manifestation: R-peak amplitude variation ±30%

**Skin Impedance Changes:**
- Baseline: 1-10 kOhm
- Increases during: Movement, dry skin, sweating onset
- Decreases during: Steady contact, moisture
- Effect on ECG: Amplitude modulation, increased noise floor
- Effect on PPG: Reduced signal quality

**Electrode Drying:**
- Rate: 0.01 kOhm/hour
- Effect: Progressive increase in impedance over hours
- Manifestation: Gradually worsening signal quality, increasing baseline wander

**Motion Artifacts:**
- Baseline wander: 0.1-0.5 Hz sinusoidal (respiratory) + Brownian walk (slow drift)
- EMG contamination: 20-250 Hz muscle noise, burst pattern
- Electrode motion: Step-function voltage jumps + exponential decay (capacitive discharge)
- Amplitude: 0.1-5.0 mV (can exceed ECG amplitude)

**Battery Voltage Effects:**
- High battery (4.0-4.2V): Normal operation
- Medium battery (3.5-4.0V): Slight gain reduction
- Low battery (3.0-3.5V): Significant gain reduction, increased noise floor
- Critical (<3.0V): Sensor failure, data corruption

**Sensor Saturation:**
- Occurs when signal exceeds ADC range
- Manifestation: Clipping at maximum/minimum values
- More likely with: High BMI (reduced dynamic range), vigorous activity

**Compression Artifacts:**
- Lossy compression at 4:1 to 10:1 ratio
- Manifestation: Loss of high-frequency detail, ringing artifacts
- Affects: QRS morphology, ST segment analysis

### 2.3.9 Synchronized Multimodal Generation

All modalities MUST be generated from the same underlying physiological state at each timepoint:
- ECG R-peaks define cardiac events
- PPG pulses follow R-peaks with pulse transit time
- Accelerometer and gyroscope reflect patient activity
- SpO2 derived from PPG
- Temperature and respiration reflect systemic state
- All modalities share the same circadian phase
- All modalities share the same activity state
- Artifact injection is cross-modally consistent (motion affects ECG, PPG, accelerometer simultaneously)

## 3.1 ECG Tokenizer Layer (tokenizer.py)

### 3.1.1 Architecture

The ECG tokenizer must compress the raw ECG signal (sampled at 130 Hz over a 5-minute window = 39,000 samples) into a sequence of temporal tokens suitable for Transformer processing.

**Requirements:**
- Input shape: (batch, ecg_samples, 1)
- Output shape: (batch, num_tokens, model_dim)
- Token reduction factor: ~100x (39,000 → ~390 tokens)
- Must preserve high-frequency diagnostic information (QRS morphology, ST segment)

**Architecture:**

```python
class ECGTokenizer(tf.keras.layers.Layer):
    """
    Multi-scale 1D convolutional tokenizer with dilated residual blocks.
    
    Architecture:
    1. Initial 1D conv: kernel=15, stride=1, filters=32 (captures fine structure)
    2. Residual Block 1: kernel=7, dilation=1, filters=64, stride=2 (reduction 2x)
    3. Residual Block 2: kernel=5, dilation=2, filters=128, stride=2 (reduction 4x)
    4. Residual Block 3: kernel=3, dilation=4, filters=256, stride=2 (reduction 8x)
    5. Residual Block 4: kernel=3, dilation=8, filters=model_dim, stride=2 (reduction 16x)
    
    Total reduction: 16x (39,000 → ~2,437 tokens)
    Additional adaptive pooling to target ~390 tokens (further 6.25x)
    
    Each residual block:
    - Dilated 1D convolution
    - Batch normalization
    - GELU activation
    - Skip connection (with 1x1 conv if dimensions change)
    - Dropout (0.1)
    """
```

**Dilated Convolution Rationale:**
- Dilation 1: Captures local QRS morphology (receptive field ~15 samples = 115 ms)
- Dilation 2: Captures PR interval, ST segment (receptive field ~35 samples = 269 ms)
- Dilation 4: Captures entire P-QRS-T complex (receptive field ~75 samples = 577 ms)
- Dilation 8: Captures beat-to-beat variability, captures rhythm (receptive field ~155 samples = 1,192 ms)
- Dilation 16: Captures multi-beat patterns (receptive field ~315 samples = 2,423 ms)

**Positional Information:**
After tokenization, add learned positional encodings to each token. Since the number of tokens varies slightly based on input length, use relative positional encoding within the Transformer rather than absolute positional encoding.

## 3.2 Cross-Attention Mechanism (attention.py)

### 3.2.1 Asymmetric Cross-Attention Layer

This is the critical fusion mechanism that allows different modalities to modulate each other.

```python
class AsymmetricCrossAttention(tf.keras.layers.Layer):
    """
    Asymmetric cross-attention where one modality serves as Query 
    and another as Key/Value.
    
    This design is motivated by clinical reasoning:
    - ECG is the primary diagnostic signal for cardiac events
    - Motion signals provide context about physical state and artifact likelihood
    - When motion is present, it should modulate (not dominate) the ECG interpretation
    
    Architecture:
    Q来自ECG tokens (primary diagnostic signal)
    K, V来自modality tokens (contextual information)
    
    This allows ECG to "query" motion context: "Is this ST change real 
    or is it motion artifact?"
    
    Multi-head attention with 8 heads, key_dim = model_dim / num_heads = 32
    
    Add residual connection and layer normalization.
    
    Apply dropout to attention weights (0.1) and value projections (0.1).
    """
    
    def call(self, ecg_tokens, context_tokens, mask=None):
        """
        Args:
            ecg_tokens: (batch, seq_len_ecg, model_dim)
            context_tokens: (batch, seq_len_context, model_dim)
            mask: Optional attention mask
            
        Returns:
            attended_tokens: (batch, seq_len_ecg, model_dim)
            
        The output maintains the ECG sequence length, with each ECG token
        now containing information about the corresponding temporal context
        from the modality.
        """
```

### 3.2.2 Hierarchical Cross-Attention

Implement multi-level fusion:
1. **Early Fusion (Token Level):** Cross-attention between corresponding temporal windows
2. **Late Fusion (Sequence Level):** Cross-attention between full sequences
3. **Global Fusion (Pooled Level):** Cross-attention between global context vectors

```python
class HierarchicalCrossAttention(tf.keras.layers.Layer):
    """
    Three-level hierarchical cross-attention fusion:
    
    Level 1 - Local Cross-Attention:
    - Each ECG token attends to its local temporal neighborhood in motion tokens
    - Window size: 5-10 tokens
    - Captures: Local artifact detection, signal quality assessment
    
    Level 2 - Global Cross-Attention:
    - Each ECG token attends to all motion tokens
    - Captures: Global activity state, physiological context
    
    Level 3 - Pooled Cross-Attention:
    - Global average pooled ECG attends to global average pooled motion
    - Single context vector
    - Captures: High-level modality relationship
    
    Final combination: Concatenation or addition of all three levels.
    """
```

### 3.2.3 Relative Positional Encoding

```python
class RelativePositionalEncoding(tf.keras.layers.Layer):
    """
    Implements relative positional encoding for the Transformer.
    
    Uses the ALiBi (Attention with Linear Biases) approach:
    - No explicit positional encodings added to tokens
    - Instead, attention scores are biased based on relative position
    - bias = -m * |i - j| where m is head-specific slope
    
    For multi-head attention with h heads:
    m_i = 2^(-8i/h) for i = 1, 2, ..., h
    
    Advantages:
    - Handles variable-length sequences naturally
    - No maximum position limit
    - Simple and effective
    - Extrapolates well to longer sequences
    """
```

## 3.3 Static Patient Embedding

```python
class StaticPatientEmbedding(tf.keras.layers.Layer):
    """
    Maps static patient demographics, medications, comorbidities, 
    and lab values to a single context embedding.
    
    Architecture:
    1. Separate MLPs for each feature group:
       - Demographics MLP: (demographic_dim → 32 → static_embedding_dim)
       - Medications MLP: (num_meds → 32 → static_embedding_dim)
       - Comorbidities MLP: (num_comorbidities → 32 → static_embedding_dim)
       - Lab Values MLP: (num_labs → 32 → static_embedding_dim)
    
    2. Combine via addition: combined = sum of all group embeddings
    
    3. Project to context token: final = Linear(combined)
    
    4. Output: Single context token of shape (batch, 1, static_embedding_dim)
    
    This context token is prepended to the sequence of temporal tokens,
    providing the model with patient-specific prior information.
    
    Design rationale:
    - Prepending a context token allows all subsequent self-attention 
      layers to attend to patient demographics
    - This is analogous to how a clinician uses patient history to 
      interpret ECG findings
    - A 70-year-old male with diabetes and prior MI has different 
      baseline ECG expectations than a 25-year-old athlete
    """
```

### 3.4.1 Transformer Encoder Block

```python
class TransformerEncoderBlock(tf.keras.layers.Layer):
    """
    Standard Transformer encoder block with pre-norm architecture.
    
    Architecture (Pre-LN Transformer):
    1. Layer Norm → Multi-Head Self-Attention → Residual Connection
    2. Layer Norm → Feed-Forward Network → Residual Connection
    
    Pre-norm is preferred over post-norm for:
    - More stable training (especially with deep networks)
    - Better gradient flow
    - Faster convergence
    
    Self-Attention:
    - num_heads: 8
    - key_dim: 32 (model_dim / num_heads = 256 / 8)
    - value_dim: 32
    - attention_dropout: 0.1
    - dropout: 0.1
    - Use relative positional encoding (ALiBi) instead of absolute positional encoding
    
    Feed-Forward Network:
    - Linear(model_dim → feedforward_dim) where feedforward_dim = 4 * model_dim = 1024
    - GELU activation (not ReLU - GELU provides smoother gradients)
    - Dropout (0.1)
    - Linear(feedforward_dim → model_dim)
    - Dropout (0.1)
    
    Layer Normalization:
    - Use epsilon = 1e-6
    - Center and scale parameters
    
    Apply dropout after each sub-layer output.
    """
```

```python
class ClassificationHead(tf.keras.layers.Layer):

       (batch, seq_len, model_dim) → (batch, model_dim)

    4. Dropout(0.2)

    6. Dropout(0.1)
    7. Dense(64 → 1) with Sigmoid activation

    The output represents the probability of OHCA within the 
    pre-arrest window (default 4 hours).
    """
```

### 3.5.2 Survival Analysis Head

```python
class SurvivalAnalysisHead(tf.keras.layers.Layer):
    """
    Discrete-time survival analysis head using the 
    framework of conditional likelihood.
    
    This head predicts the probability of OHCA at each future time bin,
    conditional on survival up to that bin.
    
    Architecture:
    1. Take CLS token or global pooled representation
    2. Dense(model_dim → 128) with GELU
    3. Dense(128 → num_survival_bins) with Sigmoid
    4. Each output S_i represents P(event in bin i | survival to bin i start)
    
    Survival bins: 12 bins of 20 minutes each = 4 hours total
    
    The cumulative survival function is:
    S(t) = product_{i=0}^{t} (1 - S_i)
    
    The probability of event in bin i is:
    P(event in bin i) = S(t_{i-1}) * S_i
    
    Loss: Negative log-likelihood of the discrete-time survival model
    (see Module 3 for loss function implementation)
    
    This provides temporal resolution: not just "will OHCA happen?" 
    but "when is it most likely to happen?"
    """
```

### 3.5.3 Uncertainty Estimation Head

```python
class UncertaintyHead(tf.keras.layers.Layer):
    """
    Estimates prediction uncertainty using Monte Carlo Dropout.
    
    During inference:
    1. Run the model N times with dropout enabled (N=50 MC samples)
    2. Collect N different predictions
    3. Compute:
       - Mean prediction: mu = (1/N) * sum(p_i)
       - Predictive uncertainty: sigma_epistemic = std(p_i) 
       - Aleatoric uncertainty: Learned via heteroscedastic output
    
    Architecture:
    1. Dense(model_dim → 64) with GELU
    2. Dense(64 → 2): outputs [mu, log_sigma]
    3. sigma = exp(log_sigma) (ensures positivity)
    4. During training: Use reparameterization trick
       p = mu + sigma * epsilon, epsilon ~ N(0,1)
    5. During inference: Use MC sampling
    
    The uncertainty estimate is critical for clinical deployment:
    - High uncertainty → flag for manual review
    - Low uncertainty with high risk → urgent alert
    - Low uncertainty with low risk → routine monitoring
    """
```

### 3.5.4 Calibration Head

```python
class CalibrationHead(tf.keras.layers.Layer):
    """
    Post-hoc calibration using temperature scaling.
    
    After training the main model, learn a single temperature parameter T:
    calibrated_prob = sigmoid(logit(raw_prob) / T)
    
    If T > 1: Model is overconfident (typical for deep networks)
    If T < 1: Model is underconfident
    If T ≈ 1: Model is well-calibrated
    
    Architecture:
    - Learnable scalar temperature parameter (initialized to 1.0)
    - Trained on validation set using NLL loss
    
    Additionally, implement Platt scaling:
    - Learn a and b parameters: calibrated_prob = sigmoid(a * logit(raw_prob) + b)
    - More flexible than temperature scaling
    """
```

## 4.1 Custom Loss Functions (losses.py)

### 4.1.1 Clinically Weighted Binary Cross-Entropy

```python
class ClinicallyWeightedBCE(tf.keras.losses.Loss):
    """
    Binary cross-entropy with asymmetric class weights.
    
    Weight matrix:
    - True Positive (OHCA correctly predicted): Weight = 1.0
    - True Negative (healthy correctly predicted): Weight = 1.0
    - False Negative (OHCA missed): Weight = 10.0 (CRITICAL: missed arrest = death)
    - False Positive (false alarm): Weight = 1.0 (less harmful, triggers review)
    
    Mathematical formulation:
    L = -[w_1 * y * log(p) + w_0 * (1-y) * log(1-p)]
    
    where w_1 = 10.0 (weight for positive class, penalizing FN)
    and w_0 = 1.0 (weight for negative class)
    
    The 10:1 ratio is chosen because:
    - Missed OHCA → certain death (no defibrillation)
    - False alarm → clinician review, appropriate response
    - In clinical practice, sensitivity > specificity is paramount
    - Literature suggests 5-20:1 ratio is appropriate for cardiac arrest detection
    """
    
    def __init__(self, fn_weight=10.0, fp_weight=1.0):
        super().__init__()
        self.fn_weight = fn_weight
        self.fp_weight = fp_weight
    
    def call(self, y_true, y_pred):
        # Clip predictions for numerical stability
        y_pred = tf.clip_by_value(y_pred, 1e-7, 1 - 1e-7)
        
        # Positive class loss (penalizes FN more)
        pos_loss = -self.fn_weight * y_true * tf.math.log(y_pred)
        
        # Negative class loss
        neg_loss = -self.fp_weight * (1 - y_true) * tf.math.log(1 - y_pred)
        
        return tf.reduce_mean(pos_loss + neg_loss)
```

### 4.1.2 Focal Loss

```python
class FocalLoss(tf.keras.losses.Loss):
    """
    Focal loss for extreme class imbalance.
    
    FL(p_t) = -alpha_t * (1 - p_t)^gamma * log(p_t)
    
    where:
    - p_t = probability of the correct class
    - gamma = focusing parameter (default 2.0)
    - alpha_t = class balancing weight
    
    When gamma = 0, focal loss = standard cross-entropy
    When gamma > 0, well-classified examples are downweighted
    Focus shifts to hard, misclassified examples
    
    For OHCA detection:
    - gamma = 2.0 (standard, empirically validated)
    - alpha_pos = 0.75 (weight for positive class)
    - alpha_neg = 0.25 (weight for negative class)
    
    Rationale: In a population of 10,000 patients, ~10 may have OHCA.
    Standard BCE is overwhelmed by the easy negatives. Focal loss
    forces the model to focus on the difficult-to-classify edge cases.
    """
```

### 4.1.3 Survival Analysis Loss

```python
class DiscreteTimeSurvivalLoss(tf.keras.losses.Loss):
    """
    Negative log-likelihood for discrete-time survival model.
    
    For each patient i:
    L_i = sum_{k=1}^{K} [delta_i(k) * log(S_i(k)) + (1-delta_i(k)) * log(1-S_i(k))]
    
    where:
    - K = number of time bins
    - delta_i(k) = 1 if event occurs in bin k, 0 otherwise
    - S_i(k) = predicted survival probability at bin k
    - log(S_i(k)) = log cumulative survival (censored contribution)
    - log(1-S_i(k)) = log hazard (event contribution)
    
    Total loss: Mean of L_i over all patients
    
    This loss:
    - Properly handles right-censored data
    - Provides temporal resolution for risk assessment
    - Is mathematically principled (maximum likelihood estimation)
    - Naturally integrates with the discrete-time survival head
    """
```

### 4.1.4 Combined Loss

```python
class CombinedOHCALoss(tf.keras.losses.Loss):

    L_total = w_cls * L_cls + w_surv * L_surv + w_aux * L_aux + w_contrast * L_contrast + w_recon * L_recon
    
    where:
    - w_cls = 1.0 (classification loss - primary objective)
    - w_surv = 0.3 (survival analysis - temporal risk)
    - w_aux = 0.1 (auxiliary physiological predictions - regularization)
    - w_contrast = 0.2 (contrastive learning - representation quality)
    - w_recon = 0.15 (reconstruction - signal understanding)
    
    The classification loss combines focal loss and clinically weighted BCE:
    L_cls = 0.5 * L_focal + 0.5 * L_weighted_bce
    """
```

```python
class CosineDecayWithWarmup(tf.keras.optimizers.schedules.LearningRateSchedule):
    """
    Learning rate schedule:
    
    Phase 1 - Linear Warmup:
    - Steps: 0 to warmup_steps (1000)
    - LR: 0 → learning_rate (1e-4)
    - Prevents early divergence with large gradients
    
    Phase 2 - Cosine Decay:
    - Steps: warmup_steps to total_steps
    - LR: learning_rate → min_learning_rate (1e-7)
    - Smooth decay that avoids local minima
    
    Mathematical form:

    """
```

### 4.2.4 Gradient Accumulation

Since memory may be limited, implement gradient accumulation:

```python
class GradientAccumulation:
    """
    Accumulate gradients over multiple mini-batches before updating.
    
    Effective batch size = micro_batch_size * accumulation_steps
    With micro_batch=8 and accumulation_steps=4: effective batch = 32
    
    Algorithm:
    1. For each micro-batch:
       - Compute gradients
       - Add to accumulated gradients (not applied)
    2. After accumulation_steps micro-batches:
       - Average accumulated gradients
       - Apply optimizer step
       - Reset accumulated gradients
    """
```

### 4.2.5 Training Step

```python
@tf.function
def train_step(model, batch, optimizer, loss_fn, accumulation_steps):
    """
    Single training step with gradient accumulation.
    
    Inputs:
    - model: OHCAPredictionModel
    - batch: dict with keys matching WindowSample fields
    - optimizer: AdamW
    - loss_fn: CombinedOHCALoss
    - accumulation_steps: int
    
    Returns:
    - loss_value: scalar tensor
    - predictions: dict of model outputs
    """
    accumulated_gradients = None
    
    for step in range(accumulation_steps):
        with tf.GradientTape() as tape:
            predictions = model(batch, training=True)
            losses = loss_fn(batch, predictions)
            total_loss = losses['total'] / accumulation_steps
        
        gradients = tape.gradient(total_loss, model.trainable_variables)
        
        if accumulated_gradients is None:
            accumulated_gradients = gradients
        else:
            accumulated_gradients = [
                ag + g for ag, g in zip(accumulated_gradients, gradients)
            ]
    
    # Average accumulated gradients
    averaged_gradients = [ag / accumulation_steps for ag in accumulated_gradients]
    
    # Clip gradients
    averaged_gradients, _ = tf.clip_by_global_norm(averaged_gradients, 1.0)
    
    # Apply gradients
    optimizer.apply_gradients(zip(averaged_gradients, model.trainable_variables))
    
    return total_loss * accumulation_steps, predictions
```

## 5.1 Metrics (metrics.py)

### 5.1.1 Primary Metrics

```python
class OHCAEvaluator:
    """
    Comprehensive evaluation suite for OHCA prediction model.
    
    All metrics computed with bootstrap confidence intervals (1000 iterations, 95% CI).
    """
    
    def compute_auroc(self, y_true, y_pred):
        """
        Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve.
        
        AUROC = integral of ROC curve
        Interpretation: Probability that a randomly chosen positive 
        is ranked higher than a randomly chosen negative.
        AUROC = 0.5 → random, AUROC = 1.0 → perfect
        
        For OHCA: AUROC > 0.85 is clinically meaningful
        AUROC > 0.95 is excellent
        
        Implementation: Use sklearn.metrics.roc_auc_score with bootstrap CI
        """
    
    def compute_auprc(self, y_true, y_pred):
        """
        Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve.
        
        More informative than AUROC for rare events (OHCA prevalence ~0.1%).
        Focuses on positive class performance.
        
        AUPRC baseline = prevalence (0.001 for OHCA)
        AUPRC > 0.1 is excellent for such rare events
        
        Implementation: Use sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score with bootstrap CI
        """
    
    def compute_sensitivity_at_threshold(self, y_true, y_pred, threshold):
        """
        Sensitivity (True Positive Rate, Recall) at a given threshold.
        
        Sensitivity = TP / (TP + FN) = proportion of OHCA cases detected
        
        CRITICAL: For OHCA, sensitivity > 0.95 is desired
        Missing an OHCA event (FN) leads to death
        False positives (FP) lead to unnecessary investigation but no harm
        
        Clinical threshold: 0.20 (deliberately low to maximize sensitivity)
        """
    
    def compute_specificity_at_threshold(self, y_true, y_pred, threshold):
        """
        Specificity (True Negative Rate) at a given threshold.
        
        Specificity = TN / (TN + FP) = proportion of healthy correctly identified
        
        For OHCA: Specificity > 0.80 is acceptable
        High sensitivity is prioritized over specificity
        
        Implementation: sklearn.metrics.confusion_matrix
        """
    
    def compute_ppv(self, y_true, y_pred, threshold):
        """
        Positive Predictive Value (Precision).
        
        PPV = TP / (TP + FP) = proportion of positive predictions that are true
        
        For rare events (prevalence 0.1%):
        Even with high sensitivity and specificity, PPV may be low
        PPV = (sensitivity * prevalence) / 
              (sensitivity * prevalence + (1-specificity) * (1-prevalence))
        
        This is a fundamental challenge in rare event prediction.
        """
    
    def compute_npv(self, y_true, y_pred, threshold):
        """
        Negative Predictive Value.
        
        NPV = TN / (TN + FN) = proportion of negative predictions that are true
        
        For OHCA: NPV should be very high (>0.999)
        If model says "low risk," the patient should truly be low risk
        """
    
    def compute_f1_score(self, y_true, y_pred, threshold):
        """
        F1 Score = 2 * PPV * Sensitivity / (PPV + Sensitivity)
        
        Harmonic mean of precision and recall.
        Balanced metric when both FP and FN matter.
        """
    
    def compute_brier_score(self, y_true, y_pred):
        """
        Brier Score = (1/N) * sum((y_pred - y_true)^2)
        
        Measures calibration quality (accuracy of probability estimates).
        Range: [0, 1], lower is better.
        BS = 0: Perfect calibration
        BS = 0.25: No skill (for balanced classes)
        BS = 1.0: Worst possible
        
        Decomposition:
        BS = Reliability - Resolution + Uncertainty
        
        Reliability: How close predicted probabilities match observed frequencies
        Resolution: How much predictions vary across different outcomes
        Uncertainty: Inherent unpredictability of the outcome
        """
```

### 5.1.2 Calibration Metrics

```python
def compute_expected_calibration_error(y_true, y_pred, n_bins=10):
    """
    Expected Calibration Error (ECE).
    
    ECE = sum_{b=1}^{B} (n_b / N) * |accuracy(b) - confidence(b)|
    
    where:
    - B = number of bins (default 10)
    - n_b = number of samples in bin b
    - N = total number of samples
    - accuracy(b) = fraction of positive samples in bin b
    - confidence(b) = mean predicted probability in bin b
    
    ECE = 0: Perfect calibration
    ECE < 0.05: Well-calibrated
    ECE > 0.10: Poorly calibrated, needs recalibration
    
    Implementation: Sort predictions into bins by predicted probability,
    compute accuracy in each bin, weight by bin size.
    """
```

### 5.1.3 Decision Curve Analysis

```python
def decision_curve_analysis(y_true, y_pred, thresholds=None):
    """
    Decision Curve Analysis (DCA).
    
    Net Benefit = (TP/N) - (FP/N) * (threshold / (1 - threshold))
    
    where:
    - TP = true positives, FP = false positives, N = total samples
    - threshold = probability threshold for intervention
    
    Compare against:
    1. Treat All: Assume everyone has OHCA (Net Benefit = prevalence - (1-prevalence) * threshold/(1-threshold))
    2. Treat None: Assume no one has OHCA (Net Benefit = 0)
    3. Perfect prediction: Upper bound
    
    Clinical utility: Model is useful when its net benefit exceeds both 
    "treat all" and "treat none" strategies across a range of clinically 
    relevant thresholds (0.01 to 0.30 for OHCA)
    
    Implementation:
    1. Vary threshold from 0.01 to 0.99
    2. Compute net benefit at each threshold
    3. Plot DCA curves
    4. Find threshold range where model is superior
    """
```

### 5.1.4 Bootstrap Confidence Intervals

```python
def bootstrap_confidence_interval(metric_fn, y_true, y_pred, 
                                   n_iterations=1000, confidence=0.95):
    """
    Bootstrap confidence intervals for any metric.
    
    Algorithm:
    1. For i in range(n_iterations):
       a. Sample with replacement: idx = random.choice(N, N)
       b. Compute metric: m_i = metric_fn(y_true[idx], y_pred[idx])
    2. Sort metrics: m_sorted = sort(m_1, ..., m_n)
    3. CI lower = m_sorted[floor(alpha/2 * n)]
    4. CI upper = m_sorted[floor((1-alpha/2) * n)]
    
    Returns: (point_estimate, ci_lower, ci_upper)
    
    This provides honest uncertainty estimates for all metrics,
    accounting for finite sample size.
    """
```

# FINAL IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

## Critical Design Decisions and Justifications

1. **Why use latent physiological states instead of direct label assignment?**
   Because OHCA is the culmination of complex physiological deterioration. Direct label assignment creates a classification problem. Latent state modeling creates a predictive model that understands the underlying physiology, leading to better generalization and more interpretable predictions.

2. **Why use asymmetric cross-attention (ECG queries, motion keys/values)?**
   Because ECG is the primary diagnostic signal for cardiac events. Motion signals provide context (activity state, artifact likelihood) but should not dominate. The asymmetric design ensures ECG features lead the interpretation while motion features modulate confidence.

3. **Why use pre-norm Transformer instead of post-norm?**
   Pre-norm Transformers train more stably, especially with deep networks and mixed precision. The gradient flow is more predictable, and convergence is faster. This is critical for a 6-layer encoder processing long sequences.

4. **Why use ALiBi relative positional encoding instead of sinusoidal?**
   ALiBi extrapolates better to variable-length sequences and doesn't require a maximum position parameter. Since different patients and windows produce different sequence lengths, extrapolation capability is essential.

5. **Why a 10:1 false-negative to false-positive weight ratio?**
   Because missed OHCA events lead to death, while false alarms lead to unnecessary investigation. The asymmetry of consequences demands asymmetric loss weighting. A 10:1 ratio is conservative; literature suggests 5-20:1 for cardiac arrest detection.

6. **Why use discrete-time survival analysis instead of binary classification?**
   Because OHCA risk is not binary at a single timepoint. It's a temporal process with varying intensity. Survival analysis provides: (a) temporal resolution (when is risk highest?), (b) proper handling of censored data, (c) integration with clinical decision-making (when to intervene?).

7. **Why implement Monte Carlo Dropout for uncertainty?**
   Because clinical deployment requires knowing when the model is uncertain. MC Dropout provides epistemic uncertainty (model uncertainty from limited data) at minimal computational overhead. This enables the system to flag cases for manual review.

8. **Why use curriculum learning?**
   Because with extreme class imbalance (0.1% OHCA prevalence), the model initially struggles to find signal. Curriculum learning starts with clear cases to establish basic patterns, then introduces harder cases. This improves convergence speed and final performance.

9. **Why implement extensive negative cohorts (LBBB, pacemaker, AF, etc.)?**
   Because these conditions produce ECG/sensor patterns that superficially resemble pre-arrest states. Without hard negatives, the model learns superficial features (e.g., wide QRS = high risk) instead of true discriminative patterns. Hard negatives force the model to learn the difference between stable pathology and acute deterioration.

10. **Why require the LLM to include safety disclaimers?**
    Because regulatory requirements (FDA, CE marking) mandate that AI systems provide decision support, not autonomous decisions. The LLM must never be perceived as replacing clinical judgment. All outputs must be clearly labeled as advisory.

```python
       - If accelerometer unavailable: Proceed with ECG only

       - Excessive artifacts: Increase uncertainty estimate

       - SpO2 < 50%: Flag as likely sensor error
       - Temperature < 30°C or > 42°C: Flag as measurement error
    
    4. Model Failure:
       - NaN in predictions: Return default risk score with high uncertainty
       - Timeout: Return last known prediction
       - Memory error: Reduce batch size and retry
    
    5. Data Integrity:
       - Corrupted TFRecord: Skip and log
       - Missing fields: Use defaults and log warning
       - Duplicate records: Remove and log
    
    6. Network Failure (LLM Orchestrator):
       - Retry with exponential backoff
       - If all retries fail: Log alert locally
       - Never block main prediction pipeline on LLM failure
```
